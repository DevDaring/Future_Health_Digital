# WASL Model Card

**Models:** drop-off risk classifier (LightGBM + isotonic calibration) and T-learner uplift model (two LightGBM classifiers).
**Owner:** WASL clinical AI team (demo). **Status:** research prototype on synthetic data.

## Intended use
Rank people with an abnormal screening result for **human-led** outreach under a daily budget, and suggest which actionable outreach package (contact channel, language-matched contact, slot type, cost information) may help. Decision support for care coordinators.

## Out of scope
* Diagnosis, triage of symptoms or treatment decisions.
* Denying, delaying or rationing care for anyone — the ranking only orders *extra* outreach.
* Showing risk scores to patients.
* Use on real patients without local validation, ethics approval and Department of Health (Abu Dhabi) data approvals.

## Training data
Synthetic only (see `DATA_CARD.md`): a seeded generator of a Gulf-city population and a simulated historical trial in which 50 % of past loops received a *randomly chosen* outreach package. Randomisation is what makes uplift identifiable. No real patient data.

## Features
Age, sex, preferred language, health-literacy level, occupation, shift pattern, distance, insurance tier, prior no-shows, smartphone, preferred channel, program, severity, days since result, language-mismatch flag, weekday/weekend availability. Action features (uplift model only): channel, language-matched, slot type, cost information.
**Excluded:** nationality (used only to audit fairness), names and all identifiers.

## Method
* **Risk:** LightGBM on the no-outreach arm, target = did not attend within the SLA; isotonic calibration on the validation split (equivalent to sklearn's prefit isotonic calibration).
* **Uplift:** T-learner; `m0` on no-outreach loops, `m1` on outreach loops with action features. Uplift = P(return | best actionable package) − P(return | no outreach). Shallow, strongly regularised trees (8 leaves, ≥150 samples per leaf) because a T-learner subtracts two models and their variance adds.
* **Explanations:** SHAP TreeExplainer on the risk model, top 5 per loop with plain-English labels.
* **Counterfactuals:** 48 actionable packages scored by `m1`; top 3 by return probability, simpler packages preferred within 1 pp.

## Evaluation
Qini/AUUC are implemented by hand (`app/ml/metrics.py`). Because the data are synthetic, each person's *true* uplift is known; the acceptance metric is AUUC against the true effect (random ≈ 0). The observed Qini on simulated outcomes is reported too; it is noisier.

## Fairness
Per-group AUROC, Brier score and true-positive rate of the high-risk flag are stored with the model and shown live on the Fairness page, alongside closure rates by group and budget share vs need share. The `uplift_fair` policy guarantees every language group at least `need_share × (1 − ε)` of the daily budget.

## Limitations
* Ground truth is synthetic; real behaviour will differ and the models must be retrained on local data (the pilot design randomises rollout for exactly this reason).
* Uplift estimates are only as good as the randomisation in the historical data.
* Calibration is on a synthetic validation split.
* Counterfactuals are model predictions, not causal guarantees for an individual.
